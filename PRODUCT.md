# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Established and premium homeowners in the Bay Area I-680 corridor: Fremont, Newark, Milpitas, Union City, Hayward, San Ramon, Dublin, Pleasanton, Danville, Walnut Creek, Concord. Typically professionals and empty-nesters with the means to invest $20K to $60K+ in a complete outdoor living space, not just a small turf install.

Their context: they are researching a significant home investment, often comparing several design-build firms, and they are skeptical. They want proof of taste and craft before they will trust someone in their backyard. The job to be done is to find a design-build partner they believe will get the details right, then book a free design consultation as a low-risk first step.

## Product Purpose

Visionable Landscaping is a Bay Area outdoor living design-build firm: 3D design first, then build. The website exists to win higher-budget projects by positioning Visionable as a premium, design-led builder rather than a commodity contractor, and to convert qualified premium homeowners into free design consultations.

Success is not raw traffic or volume of small jobs. Success is a steady flow of high-intent consultation requests from homeowners who already perceive the brand as premium before they ever pick up the phone.

## Brand Personality

Refined and confident. Quiet luxury: understated, assured, design-led. The brand signals craftsmanship and expertise without shouting.

Three words: refined, confident, visionary.

Voice is vision-led and warm but elevated, never folksy or hard-sell. It speaks the buyer's language, not the trade's: "outdoor room" not "hardscape project," "create" and "build" not "install," "live in it" not "complete the job." The recurring "Vision" thread (We Make It Visionable, Share Your Vision, See It Before It's Real) is brand spine, woven in naturally, never repeated as a slogan.

Emotional goal: a premium homeowner should finish a page thinking "these people have taste and will get it right."

## Anti-references

Generic home-services contractor sites. Specifically avoid:

- Clip-art service icons and stock "happy crew" photography.
- Loud "FREE QUOTE" banners, countdown urgency, and hard-sell pricing blocks.
- Badge soup: rows of trust seals, review-platform logos, and certification clutter competing for attention.
- Crowded, busy layouts that read as a default template or a bargain operator.

Anything that makes the brand feel cheap, interchangeable, or templated undercuts the premium positioning and pushes away the high-budget buyer.

## Design Principles

1. **Show the vision, don't sell the service.** Lead with what life looks like in the finished space. Imagery and outcomes do the persuading; feature lists come second.
2. **Premium is restraint.** Quiet confidence beats loud persuasion. Generous space, a few decisive elements, and one clear call to action signal more credibility than badges and banners ever could.
3. **Proof over promises.** This buyer is skeptical and investing heavily. Real projects, honest before-and-afters, and recognizable Bay Area homes earn more trust than adjectives.
4. **Speak human, not contractor.** Frame everything around the homeowner's life and how they will use the space, never around materials, trade jargon, or process for its own sake.
5. **Every screen leads to a conversation.** The free design consultation is the conversion. Make the path to it obvious and inviting on every page, without pressure or repetition.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA. Maintain the basics already in place (semantic HTML, skip-to-content link, prefers-reduced-motion handling, lang attribute) and hold new work to:

- Color contrast at AA, with particular care where forest green (#1B6B2A) or marigold (#E8960C) sit on warm light surfaces or carry text.
- Keyboard navigability with visible focus states on all interactive elements.
- Descriptive alt text on every project and portfolio image, since imagery carries the brand.
- No motion or interaction that depends on hover or animation alone to convey meaning.
